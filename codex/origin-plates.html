Plate I

The Standard Origin

Big Bang cosmology → General Relativity → the unified field theory quest → where it stalled

This is the paradigm most of physics still stands on: a single hot, dense origin (the Big Bang), described by General Relativity, with four separate forces that have never been fully reconciled into one framework. Einstein spent the last three decades of his life trying to close that gap — and didn't.

In honor of the reach, not just the stall
1915

General Relativity

Einstein publishes the field equations of gravity as spacetime curvature — the theory that would anchor everything he tried to build on top of it.

1920s

The unification quest begins

With only two known forces at the time — gravity and electromagnetism — Einstein sets out to melt them into a single field theory. He borrows Kaluza-Klein's idea of extra spacetime dimensions to try to make the math close.

1920s–1955

Pure mathematics, resistant to quantum mechanics

Einstein's approach stays almost entirely mathematical — variations on General Relativity's geometry — while he remains skeptical of quantum mechanics as a fundamental description of nature, rather than incorporating it.

Mid-20th c.

Two more forces appear

Physicists discover the strong and weak nuclear forces. Einstein's entire framework was built for a two-force universe — the ground shifts under the theory before it's finished.

1955

Einstein dies, unification unresolved

He never completes a unified field theory. Electromagnetism and the weak force are unified in 1968 — thirteen years after his death, and by a route he never took.

Today

The gap is patched, not closed

Modern cosmology accounts for the mismatch between what's observed and what General Relativity alone predicts by adding dark matter (~27%) and dark energy (~68%) — unseen, unexplained placeholders rather than a resolved unification.

Where he got stuck

Einstein tried to unify gravity and electromagnetism through pure geometry, without a topological mechanism for confinement or charge — and without room for the two forces discovered mid-quest. The math could describe curvature; it had no native way to explain why matter holds together, why charge is quantized, or why four forces should ever have been one to begin with. That is the wall the current paradigm still stands in front of: the forces are described, not unified — and the missing 95% of the universe is named (dark matter, dark energy), not explained.

Red Dwarf

Belts migrate inner core → outer core → mantle; a new inner core belt forms; the remaining belts become molecular zones — these three belts will later form the Oort cloud. Nucleosynthesis begins. Morphs into a Yellow Dwarf.

Yellow Dwarf

(Not our Sun.) Nuclear fission is achieved. Two belts migrate to the mantle and outer core; a new inner core forms. Morphs into a Red Giant.

Red Giant → Super Red Giant (SRG)

Nuclear fusion is achieved; three belts migrate mantle-ward, a new inner core forms — morphs into an SRG. Fusion pushes to metallic states: three belts become the Oort Cloud, two become the Kuiper Belt, a new inner core becomes the Asteroid Belt.

Supernova event
